PROJECT 01

MCU Data Logger – ATmega328P Data Acquisition Board

2-Layer FR4 KiCad 9 Battery-Backed
Board Layers: Toggle to Inspect
Click to Expand
TOTAL EEPROM 256 KB (I2C Bus)
REAL TIME CLOCK DS1337 + Battery

Autonomous Battery-Backed Data Acquisition Unit

Engineered in KiCad 9 to function as a compact, self-contained microcontroller data logging system. It acquires sensor telemetry and securely logs timestamps with millisecond precision onto dual non-volatile EEPROM memory chips even during power failures.

Key Subsystems

ATmega328P-AU Core, Maxim DS1337 Real-Time Clock, dual 24LC1025 EEPROMs, coin-cell backup circuit, and breakout headers for I2C, UART, GPIO, and ICSP.

Design Milestones

Structured hierarchical sheets, custom library management, 100% clean ERC run, low-inductance ground pour, and production Gerber generation.

Hierarchical KiCad Schematic Design

Rather than a cluttered flat schematic, the design was organized hierarchically into clean functional sheets:

01. MCU Sheet: ATmega328P-AU power pins, reset circuit with pull-up resistor and tactile switch, 16MHz crystal with matched load capacitors, and ICSP programming header.
02. RTC Sheet: DS1337 real-time clock connected to dedicated 32.768 kHz watch crystal, coin cell diode-OR backup power path, and I2C address pins.
03. Memory Sheet: Dual 24LC1025 EEPROMs mapped with distinct hardware A0 address pins to avoid bus conflicts, with 4.7kΩ I2C pull-ups on SDA/SCL.
04. ERC Validation: Applied proper PWR_FLAG symbols to power nets, eliminated unassigned pins, and achieved a 0-error, 0-warning ERC check.

2-Layer PCB Layout & Signal Integrity Practices

Decoupling Placement

100nF ceramic decoupling capacitors positioned directly adjacent to MCU VCC and AVCC pins (< 2mm) to ensure high-frequency ripple filtering before entering the IC.

Oscillator Loop Area

16MHz crystal placed directly beside XTAL1/XTAL2 with symmetrical trace lengths and an isolated ground guard ring to suppress parasitic clock EMI.

Solid Return Path

Bottom copper layer (B.Cu) poured as a contiguous ground plane to ensure low-impedance return current loops for both digital clocks and I2C communication.

DFM & Silkscreen

Thermal reliefs configured on all ground pads for reliable hand and reflow soldering. Legible silkscreen pin labels for UART, I2C, and ICSP headers.

SafeSteps – ESP32-Based Personal Safety Device

Cellular IoT Wearable for Rapid Emergency Dispatch

SafeSteps is a battery-powered personal safety device engineered around an ESP32 SoC. It continuously monitors for emergency triggers via an SOS button; upon activation, it acquires real-time satellite coordinates from a NEO-6M GPS receiver and transmits distress alerts over cellular networks using an A7670C 4G LTE module, while triggering an onboard buzzer and status LED.

Multi-Radio Integration

Co-locating 4G LTE cellular, GPS receiver, and 2.4GHz WiFi/BLE with sufficient RF keepout zones and return path isolation.

Low-Power Battery Life

Regulated by an HT7333 ultra-low quiescent current LDO directly fed from a 3.7V Li-Po cell for extended field reliability.

Schematic Capture & Custom Symbol Engineering

Created custom KiCad symbols and footprint models directly from component datasheets for parts without stock library definitions:

01. Dual UART Channels: Configured UART1 for NEO-6M GPS communication and UART2 for AT-command parsing with the A7670C 4G modem, ensuring non-blocking concurrent data streams.
02. Power & LDO Regulation: Implemented HT7333 3.3V linear regulation with low ESR input/output tantalums and ceramic bulk capacitors to handle sudden transmitter surge currents.
03. SOS & Alert Interfaces: Hardware pull-up debouncing on emergency button to avoid false triggers; transistor-switched buzzer circuit for audible alarms without drawing excessive MCU current.
04. ERC Verification: Validated pin types (Input, Output, Power Output) to maintain strict signal integrity and prevent floating gates.

Net Classes, Trace Sizing & Layout Rules

Net Class Segmentation

Established separate KiCad Net Classes: 0.6mm-0.8mm width for Li-Po and 3.3V power rails vs 0.25mm width for high-density logic signals.

Antenna Keepout Zones

Preserved copper-free keepout zones underneath the ESP32 PCB trace antenna to avoid detuning and RF attenuation.

Thermal & Current Bursts

Poured heavy copper zones around the A7670C modem and HT7333 regulator to dissipate heat during 4G transmission bursts.

DRC & Gerber Verification

Executed DRC with manufacturing clearances (0.2mm track-to-track, 0.3mm drill size), verifying all Gerbers in KiCad Gerbview.

METHODICAL ENGINEERING APPROACH

Standard PCB Design Lifecycle

Every board follows a disciplined, repeatable engineering progression to prevent respins and guarantee first-pass bring-up.

01

Requirements & Part Selection

Component research against vendor datasheets. Calculating voltage tolerances, decoupling capacitor values, crystal load capacitance, and pull-up resistor sizing.

Datasheet Analysis
02

Hierarchical Schematic & ERC

Structuring circuits into logical sheets. Net labeling, custom symbol drafting, power flag assignment, and running Electrical Rule Checks (ERC) until 0 errors remain.

ERC Verification
03

Constraint-Driven PCB Layout

Strategic part placement (connectors on edge, decoupling next to VCC pins). Routing with defined net classes, prioritizing unbroken ground reference planes.

Signal & Power Integrity
04

DRC & Fabrication Release

Running Design Rule Check against fab house constraints. Generating RS-274X Gerbers, Excellon drill files, and silkscreen verification in Gerbview.
